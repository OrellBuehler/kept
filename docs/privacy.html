Privacy policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

In short: the developer collects nothing. There is no Kept account with the developer, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The apps talk only to the Kept server whose address you enter, which you or someone you trust runs. Your financial data lives on that server, not with the developer.

Who is responsible

Kept is developed by Orell Bühler, Switzerland. For questions about this policy, open an issue at github.com/OrellBuehler/kept. For anything private, such as a security problem, use GitHub's private vulnerability report for the repository instead of a public issue.

What the developer receives

Nothing. The developer runs no server for Kept. The apps contain no analytics, advertising, tracking or crash reporting tools and do not contact the developer. Nobody can see your accounts, bills, balances or documents except whoever operates your server.

Where your data is

The apps connect only to the Kept server whose address you enter when you first open them. That server is yours, or run by someone you trust, and its operator holds your financial data. This policy cannot speak for a server that someone else runs for you: ask its operator what they keep and who can see it.

Everything an app shows is fetched from that server when you open a screen. The apps keep no database of your accounts or transactions on the device.

What the apps store on your device

  • The address of your server.
  • The name of the signed-in user.
  • The session token that keeps you signed in. On Android it is encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore. On iPhone it is in the Keychain, readable only on this device.
  • Your reminder settings and the list of reminders already shown, so that none is shown twice.

When you open a document, such as a bill PDF or a report, the app downloads it into its cache or temporary folder so that it can be shown or handed to another app. The system may clear those folders at any time.

On Android these settings are excluded from the system's cloud backup. On iPhone the server address, user name and reminder settings can be part of a backup of the device that you make yourself; the session token cannot.

Permissions and what they are for

  • Network access: to reach your server. Nothing else is contacted.
  • Notifications: only if you switch reminders on. They are local notifications created on the device. There is no push service, and nothing is sent to Apple or Google to deliver them. A reminder names the creditor and the amount of a bill, so what shows on a locked screen follows your system's notification settings.
  • Camera: only when you choose to photograph or scan a bill. The image is turned into a PDF on the device and uploaded to your own server. On iPhone the app asks for camera access for the scanner. On Android the app does not hold the camera permission: it asks the system camera app to take the photo, saves it in its private cache folder and deletes it once it has been read.
  • Photos and files: only through the system pickers, and only the items you pick. The apps cannot browse your photo library or your files.
  • Background refresh: if reminders are on, the app asks your server in the background what is due, roughly every six hours when the system allows it, and notifies on the device.

Connections

The apps use https unless you type an address that starts with http://. That is possible for a server inside a home network, and the apps show a warning when you do. Over plain http anyone on the network path can read what is sent, including your password. Use https wherever you can.

Signing out and removing data

  • Sign out ends the session on your server (when it can be reached) and removes the session token and the user name from the device. You stay connected to the server and can sign in again.
  • Disconnect from this server also removes the server address, your reminder settings and the list of shown reminders, and cancels reminders scheduled ahead. A document you opened stays in the cache until the system clears it.
  • Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device.

None of this deletes anything on your server. Your data there stays until you remove it in Kept.

App Store, Google Play and TestFlight

Apple and Google may collect information when you download or use apps from their stores, for example installation numbers or crash reports, if you agreed to share these with developers on your device. This is covered by the Apple Privacy Policy and the Google Privacy Policy. If you send feedback through TestFlight, Apple passes on your comment, screenshot, device details and the email address of your TestFlight account to the developer.

The Kept server software

The server is software you run yourself. It sends nothing to the developer. Its optional integrations contact only the services the operator configures: Paperless-ngx, a public price service for market data (off until a user switches it on), notification channels such as ntfy, a webhook or email, and S3 storage for files. What those services receive is governed by their own policies and by how the operator has set them up.

This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and loads nothing from other companies: the fonts and images are served from the same place as the pages. It stores your choice of light or dark theme in your browser's local storage, which stays in your browser and is never sent anywhere. GitHub may log technical data such as your IP address to operate the service; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Children

Kept collects no personal data from anyone, including children.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to hand over, correct or delete on the developer's side. Your data is on your server and on your device, and you control it there.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new date. The history of this page is public in the project repository.
